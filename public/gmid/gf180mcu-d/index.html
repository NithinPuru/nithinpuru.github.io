IHP SG13G2

130 nm SiGe:C BiCMOS · IHP Microelectronics · PSP 103 · Apache 2.0

The process

SG13G2 is IHP's 130 nm SiGe:C BiCMOS technology, published as an open-source PDK under the Apache 2.0 licence. Besides the MOSFETs characterised here it offers SiGe:C npn heterojunction bipolar transistors (fT/fmax up to 350/450 GHz), MIM capacitors, polysilicon resistors and a back end of five thin aluminium layers with two thick top metals. The MOSFETs come in two gate oxides: thin-oxide LV devices for the 1.2 V core and thick-oxide HV devices for 3.3 V I/O.

The transistors use the PSP 103 surface-potential model, which IHP ships as Verilog-A; here it is compiled for ngspice with OpenVAF. The model library offers the process corners mos_tt, mos_ss, mos_ff, mos_sf and mos_fs; this explorer uses TT, SS and FF.

Devices characterised here

  • sg13_lv_nmos, sg13_lv_pmos: thin-oxide core devices, L ≥ 0.13 µm, 1.2 V (the model files give 1.5 V as the maximum drain voltage).
  • sg13_hv_nmos, sg13_hv_pmos: thick-oxide I/O devices for 3.3 V, L ≥ 0.45 µm (NMOS) and 0.40 µm (PMOS).

Worth knowing

  • The LV NMOS shows a strong reverse short-channel effect: at TT and 27 °C its Vth is at 0.13 µm but at 1 µm. Long LV devices therefore turn on at a lower VGS than short ones.
  • Intrinsic gain grows slowly with length in the LV NMOS (gm/gds at gm/ID = 10 V−1: at Lmin, at 1 µm); the LV PMOS gains more from length ( at 1 µm).
  • The thin gate oxide tunnels: at −40 °C and nanoampere currents part of the drain current of the LV devices is gate–drain tunnelling. The curves here use the channel current; see the method below.

  • IHP Open PDK documentation (process specification, layout rules)
  • Analog circuit design course by JKU Linz, built on SG13G2

SKY130A

130 nm CMOS · SkyWater Technology · BSIM4 · Apache 2.0

The process

SKY130 is SkyWater Technology's 130 nm CMOS process, open-sourced together with Google under the Apache 2.0 licence; SKY130A is the variant installed by open_pdks. Besides 1.8 V core transistors in three threshold-voltage flavours it offers 5 V and 20 V devices with extended drains, native transistors, MIM and MOS capacitors, polysilicon resistors, a local-interconnect layer and five metal layers.

The transistors use BSIM4. Current open_pdks installs SkyWater's continuous models, which were re-extracted to stay smooth across W and L; they replace the original micro-binned models, which were accurate only close to the sizes that had been measured. Corners in the library: tt, ss, ff, sf and fs (and ll, hh, hl, lh); this explorer uses TT, SS and FF.

Devices characterised here

  • nfet_01v8, pfet_01v8: standard-Vth 1.8 V devices, L ≥ 0.15 µm.
  • nfet_01v8_lvt (L ≥ 0.15 µm) and pfet_01v8_lvt (L ≥ 0.35 µm): low-Vth devices, with more drive current and more leakage.
  • pfet_01v8_hvt: high-Vth PMOS for low leakage; the 1.8 V family has no high-Vth NMOS.
  • nfet_g5v0d10v5, pfet_g5v0d10v5: 5 V gate oxide with a drain extended to 10.5 V, L ≥ 0.5 µm; characterised here at VDD = 5 V, so VDS = 2.5 V.

Worth knowing

  • Narrow transistors have a noticeably higher threshold: for nfet_01v8 at L = 0.15 µm (TT, 27 °C) Vth is about 0.77 V at W = 1 µm against 0.71 V at W = 5 µm. The per-width numbers here hold for fingers of about 5 µm.
  • Short-channel effects are strong in the PMOS: pfet_01v8 has Vth = at 0.15 µm and at 1 µm (TT, 27 °C).
  • The extended-drain devices trade speed for voltage: peak fT of nfet_g5v0d10v5 at its minimum length is , against for nfet_01v8.
  • Two models have a bump in gm/ID between weak and strong inversion, where it rises again by about 1 V−1 before falling: pfet_01v8 at its shortest lengths (|VGS| near 0.6 to 0.7 V) and nfet_g5v0d10v5 from L = 1.5 µm (near 0.73 V). The drain current is smooth there and gm matches its slope, so the bump belongs to the models; plotted against gm/ID it shows as a fold, and other quantities step where gm/ID stays nearly flat.

  • sky130_fd_pr primitive library (FOSSi Foundation; continuous models in combined_models)
  • open_pdks, which builds SKY130A

GF180MCU-D

180 nm CMOS · GlobalFoundries · BSIM4 · Apache 2.0

The process

GF180MCU is GlobalFoundries' 180 nm mixed-signal microcontroller process, open-sourced together with Google under the Apache 2.0 licence. Its MOSFETs come as 3.3 V devices and thick-oxide 5 V and 6 V devices; the letter D names one of the metal-stack options that open_pdks packages, and the transistor models are the same in every option.

The transistors use BSIM4 (version 4.5) with models binned by width and length. Corners in the library: typical, ff, ss, fs and sf, plus a statistical set; this explorer uses TT (typical), SS and FF.

Devices characterised here

  • nfet_03v3, pfet_03v3: 3.3 V devices, L ≥ 0.28 µm.
  • nfet_05v0 (L ≥ 0.6 µm) and pfet_05v0 (L ≥ 0.5 µm): 5 V devices. Their subcircuits use the 6 V model cards, so they are the 6 V transistors at a shorter minimum length and a lower supply.
  • nfet_06v0 (L ≥ 0.7 µm) and pfet_06v0 (L ≥ 0.55 µm): 6 V thick-oxide devices.
  • *_dss: drain-side silicide block. The drain and source diffusions are left unsilicided over d_sab (1.78 µm for 3.3 V; 3.78 µm for the 6 V NMOS, 2.78 µm for the 6 V PMOS) and s_sab (0.48 µm; 0.28 µm), which the model represents as resistors in series with drain and source; the extra ballast resistance is why such devices are used where ESD robustness matters. Here gm, gds and gmb of these devices are referred to the outer terminals, so they include those resistors.

Worth knowing

  • At W = 5 µm the series resistors of the DSS devices cost little: the on-current of nfet_03v3_dss at Lmin is against for nfet_03v3 (TT, 27 °C, VGS = VDD, VDS = VDD/2).
  • The 3.3 V devices reach high intrinsic gain at moderate length: nfet_03v3 at 1 µm gives gm/gds = at gm/ID = 10 V−1.

Sources

  • GF180MCU PDK documentation

  • open_pdks, which builds GF180MCU-D

How the curves were made

Every transistor of the three PDKs went through one testbench, so curves of different devices and PDKs can be compared directly. One transistor, W = 5 µm in a single finger, source and bulk at 0 V (VSB = 0), VDS = VDD/2, and VGS swept from 0 to VDD in steps of VDDnom/150. PMOS devices are driven with the mirrored voltages and shown as magnitudes.

Process
TT, SS and FF corners of each PDK's model library: global (die-to-die) variation. Local mismatch is not included.
Supply
0.9, 1.0 and 1.1 times the device's nominal VDD. The supply sets VDS = VDD/2 and the end of the VGS sweep.
Temperature
−40, 27 and 125 °C.
Lengths
Lmin, then 0.18, 0.25, 0.35, 0.5, 0.7, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 5 µm, from 1.15 Lmin upwards.
Quantities
ID, gm, gds, gmb, Vth and VDS,sat from the model's operating point. Cgg, Cgd and Cgs from small-signal AC solutions at 1 MHz at every bias point, so they include the gate overlap and fringe capacitances that the models' operating-point capacitances leave out. fT = gm/(2πCgg).
Low currents
ID is the channel current. Below 10 nA/µm, where the reported gm/ID differs from the slope d ln ID/dVGS by more than 2 %, the slope is used (it happens in the thin-oxide IHP devices at −40 °C, where PSP's gm includes gate–drain tunnelling). Points below 1 fA/µm, the simulator's numerical floor, are left out.
Not modelled
Layout effects (diffusion length, well proximity), narrow-width effects at widths far from 5 µm per finger, and interconnect parasitics.
Storage
567 tables (21 devices × 27 conditions) of 16-bit values, resolution better than 0.1 %, about 20 KB each and loaded only when shown.

The script that runs the simulations, gmid/characterise.py, and the setup script that fetches the pinned model files are in this site's repository, so every curve can be regenerated.
